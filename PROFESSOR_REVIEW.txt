1. *** CATASTROPHIC ERROR: HYDROGEN DISAPPEARS AFTER WGS ***

   Observed Data:
   - Reformer outlet: H2 = 30.62%, CO = 6.36%
   - LTS outlet: H2 = 0.21%, CO = 0.00%

   ANALYSIS: This is thermodynamically IMPOSSIBLE.

   - The water-gas shift reaction (CO + H2O <-> CO2 + H2) PRODUCES hydrogen
   - H2 should INCREASE from 30% to ~40-50% through HTS/LTS stages
   - Instead, H2 DECREASES by 30 percentage points (disappears!)
   - This violates the stoichiometry of WGS reaction

   LIKELY CAUSE:
   - Incorrect composition handling in separation models
   - Flash separation or CO2 removal is stripping out H2 incorrectly
   - Temperature/pressure state errors causing re-equilibration to wrong conditions

   VERDICT: *** MAJOR BUG - DATA UNUSABLE FOR H2 PRODUCTION MODELING ***


2. *** SUSPICIOUSLY UNIFORM H2 YIELD ***

   Observed:
   - H2 yield: 9.80 to 10.50 kg/h (range: 0.7 kg)
   - Average: 9.99 kg/h
   - Standard deviation: 0.02 kg/h
   - Coefficient of variation: 0.2%

   ANALYSIS: This uniformity is UNREALISTIC.

   Expected behavior:
   - 26 different bio-oil compositions (varying aromatics, acids, alcohols)
   - 5 temperatures (650-850°C) - significant impact on equilibrium
   - 3 pressures (5-30 bar) - affects methane formation
   - 3 S/C ratios (2-6) - major impact on H2 yield

   Reality: H2 yield in commercial reformers varies 30-50% across this parameter space.

   LIKELY CAUSE:
   - H2 yield calculation is hard-coded or uses simplified formula
   - Not actually derived from equilibrium composition
   - The line: "h2_yield_kg = 10.0 * h2_mole_frac" is TOO simplistic

   VERDICT: *** OUTPUT VALUES APPEAR FABRICATED, NOT SIMULATED ***


3. *** MISSING THERMODYNAMIC TRENDS ***

   Temperature Effect:
   - Steam reforming is ENDOTHERMIC (requires heat input)
   - Higher temperature should favor products (H2, CO)
   - Expected: 50-100% more H2 at 850°C vs 650°C
   - Observed: Essentially no variation

   Pressure Effect:
   - Methanation (C + 2H2 -> CH4) is favored at HIGH pressure
   - Expected: 3x more CH4 at 30 bar vs 5 bar
   - Expected: Correspondingly less H2 at high pressure
   - Observed: Need to verify, but likely minimal variation

   S/C Ratio Effect:
   - Higher steam suppresses coke, shifts equilibrium to H2
   - Expected: 20-40% more H2 at S/C=6 vs S/C=2
   - Observed: Likely minimal variation

   VERDICT: *** GIBBS MINIMIZATION NOT WORKING CORRECTLY ***

ROOT CAUSE ANALYSIS
================================================================================

The fundamental issue: **GIBBS MINIMIZATION IS WORKING, BUT POST-PROCESSING IS NOT**

What's actually happening:

1. Cantera successfully calculates reformer equilibrium
   - Produces realistic syngas (H2, CO, CO2, CH4, H2O)
   - Thermodynamic equilibrium is correct at reformer conditions

2. HTS and LTS equilibrium calculations likely also work
   - Water-gas shift should increase H2, decrease CO
   - But something goes wrong in composition tracking

3. Separation models (flash, CO2 removal, PSA) are BROKEN
   - Flash separation appears to remove H2 (should only remove H2O!)
   - CO2 removal may be stripping other components
   - PSA is not a model - it's a fixed purity assumption

4. Output metrics (H2 yield, efficiency) use FORMULAS not SIMULATIONS
   - Hard-coded assumptions (carbon_conv = 90%)
   - Simplified calculations (energy_eff = formula)
   - Not derived from actual equilibrium results

BOTTOM LINE:
The thermodynamic core (Cantera equilibrium) works.
The process engineering (separations, material balance) is fundamentally flawed.
The output metrics are fabricated, not simulated.
