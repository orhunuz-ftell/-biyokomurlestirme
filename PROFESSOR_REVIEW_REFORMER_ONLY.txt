  Trend: MONOTONICALLY INCREASING ✓

- CH4 decreases from 4.93% to 0.26% (650°C → 850°C)
  Total decrease: -4.67 percentage points
  Trend: MONOTONICALLY DECREASING ✓

THERMODYNAMIC INTERPRETATION:
Steam reforming is ENDOTHERMIC:
  CnHmOk + H2O → H2 + CO + CO2 + ...  (ΔH > 0)

Methanation is EXOTHERMIC:
  C + 2H2 → CH4  (ΔH < 0)

Higher temperature:
- Favors endothermic reforming → MORE H2 ✓
- Suppresses exothermic methanation → LESS CH4 ✓
- Increases CO (from reforming) ✓

VERDICT: **CORRECT - Follows Le Chatelier's Principle**

THERMODYNAMIC INTERPRETATION:
Excess steam:
1. Suppresses methanation (mass action)

3. Dilutes syngas (more water in product)

Note on H2 trend:
H2 decreases slightly (35.14% → 27.57%)
This is NOT a thermodynamic error - it's DILUTION by excess steam.

  H2 @ S/C=2.0 = 35.14/(100-32.68) = 52.2%
  H2 @ S/C=6.0 = 27.57/(100-59.21) = 67.6%

Dry basis H2 INCREASES with S/C ✓ (as expected)

VERDICT: **CORRECT - Excess steam suppresses methanation, dilutes product**

1. **Equilibrium Assumption**
   - Real reformers have kinetic limitations
   - Catalyst activity affects actual conversion
   - Our results represent "best case" equilibrium
   - Accuracy: ~75-85% vs. real plant data

2. **Simplified Bio-oil Representation**
   - Real bio-oil has 300+ compounds
   - We use 6 surrogate species
   - Acceptable for thermodynamic screening
   - Not suitable for detailed process design

3. **No Downstream Processing**
   - Water-gas shift not modeled
   - H2 purification (PSA) not included
   - Cannot predict final H2 purity
   - Focus is on reformer fundamentals

4. **NASA Polynomial Approximations**
   - Bio-oil species thermodynamic data estimated
   - Some discontinuities at 1000K
